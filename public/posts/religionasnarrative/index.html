Human Domestication as Recorded by the Bible

October 6, 2024 · 6 min

If you’ve read my other past regarding our conscious experience as that of post-hoc rationalisation of what is effectively a deterministic existence then firstly thank you, and secondly if not then it might be good to read that before reading this.

Consciousness and religion are two of my favourite fields to think about. I would probably class myself as somewhere between an atheist and a Christian agnostic depending on the day or season. One of the great gifts of recent years intellectually has been Jordan Peterson’s lecture series on the psychological truths of the Old Testament texts. This opened a world of questioning and curiosity for me.

Backing for idea that Old Testament is a record of the evolution of human consciousness#

  • Think Garden of Eden through the fall into agriculture and society. From these, one of the things that becomes apparent is certainly the book of Genesis can be seen as the self-consciousness awakening of humanity.

Human Domestication Theory#

Though maligned as uncool now, Sapiens by Yuval Noah Harrari provided an important framing of the process of how we moved from hunter-gatherers to those of civilisation. In that, the idea that it wasn’t that humans domesticated wheat, but in fact wheat that domesticated humans. We became their carers, tending to their needs from planting, watering, protection from pests through to harvest and spreading of their DNA. We became tied to their survival in a symbiotic though not overly healthy way for humans considering the relative drop in nutritional value provided by grains.

However, it was a one-way ticket. The increased calories available from agriculture led to a boom of human populations from which there was no return. Land was given over to crops that would have once been wild so now not only was there more humans than could have been supported but there was no way to reverse the trend even if you wanted to return to nature.

Once we were immobile and reliant on single crop sources, our existence became more precarious and rival tribes who would have always been a cause for concern could easily steal or wipe out a year’s worth of food easily. The rise of the warrior class who protected the territory and supported by the excess grain produced was the first step towards a stratified society that we know.

And in this process we see humans reflecting on their plight more clearly. Firstly at the hands of the capricious nature of the weather disease but also to the attempt to understand the feasts and famines that nature would provide. There came the need for cooperation between people like never before and the ideas of how to structure and run such a society became very pertinent.

Each Prophet revealed how better to live within a society#

The codification of the Law of Moses can be considered one of the earliest rule books for how to get along in a Bronze Age society. The issues of jealousy, theft and violence were addressed and given the authority of God himself. These laws may seem self-evident now, but that goes to show how well ingrained they have become.

Each of the Old Testament prophets could be considered to be revealing deeper and more nuanced truths of how to cooperate and ensure harmony amongst more and more complicated societal setups.

The revelation of God through the history of Israel#

The idea that the Jews were and are a chosen people is contentious to say the least. One framing that could be considered more neutral might be that in hindsight it is as if God has been revealing himself through their society. This of course presupposes that God is thought of in the broadest possible terms as all of reality and the reasoning for the rises and falls of societies i.e. that He is that is revealed as successful. Through the trials and tribulations of the Jewish people they have seen the ups and downs of life which reveal God’s presence. They are not static and have evolved through time making them perhaps uniquely positioned being recipients of the underlying truths of reality that are present. In capturing these revelations through text and transmitting them as a religion they chart the development and refinement of a culture as it amasses more people and finds itself in more difficult circumstances.

The unique insights of Christ above and beyond Old Testament teachings#

On a slightly tangential note, the issue of Jesus cannot be avoided. I say issue as all of Western culture hangs on his teachings and fall subsequent to the spread of the religion founded in his name. Additionally, the insights he espoused regarding the right treatment of those we share the world with have been foundational to the concept of human rights and all things that, like the 10 Commandments, seem self evident to us now.

Of course, it’s not just a moral teaching and philosophy that discussion of him leads to. It is the claims made by him and his followers of his literal existence and miracles. Most notable of those would have to be his resurrection. I’ll take an unsurprisingly heretical approach and state that I do not believe that that occurred. At least not as it was said to have happened.

However, that does leave the rather difficult topic of what to do about those who claim to have witnessed his resurrection. Whilst these could be discounted out of hand such as that of the ravings of madmen, I don’t think that is a fair position to take. Firstly, the weight of persecution that would be levelled at his followers (spoiler: all of the disciples are eventually killed) makes clear that it was no trivial matter to claim belief or witness to such events. Secondly, the range of witnesses from women to sensible tax collectors along with members of the wider public (as claimed) again make such claims difficult.

Another issue is that he was unrecognised by those who knew him well. If we believe he was bodily resurrected then it was not a very convincing reanimation if it left him unrecognisable. He was known to have brothers, was it perhaps one of them standing in for him?

Or, perhaps, it was what Jesus revealed during his life that was transcendent to those who heard it. The truths of extending forgiveness and compassion to all those around you and not just those of your kind is still revolutionary today if enacted. The idea of the long wait for the Kingdom of God to be built here in Israel being dispelled but instead it being a spiritual kingdom that you, yes you poor peasant, could inhabit now must have been stunning too. Perhaps all of these meant that he as a person transcended death and his appearance as a spirit to many of his followers was the echoing and setting in of the teachings they had learnt whilst he was alive but not really resonated until digesting his death.

As with any good mystery, we’ll never know. But it does provide some pointers to us today as to what this thing we call the Bible actually is. It’s a record of our own journey from walking in abundant paradise through the fall of miserable servitude of our agricultural existence to the psychological insight of being aware of our own thoughts (given precedence to action as detailed in the Sermon on the Mount) that lead us to the technological ingenuity, and its own enslavement, of recent times.
